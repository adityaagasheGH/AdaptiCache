A long scan should not cost you your hot pages.

LRU throws out pages you use every second to make room for pages you will never touch again. AdaptiCache remembers what it evicted, notices when it got that wrong, and shifts its own balance.

Each classic policy bets on one assumption

When the workload breaks that assumption, the policy keeps making the same mistake. These three small runs come straight from the engines in this project.

FIFO

Assumes the oldest page is the least useful. It never looks at how pages are used, and can get worse when you give it more memory.

LRU

Assumes the recent past predicts the near future. A one-time scan looks recent, so it pushes out the three pages this program uses most.

OPT

Makes the fewest faults possible by evicting the page needed furthest in the future. No operating system knows the future, so OPT is the yardstick, not a policy you can run.

How AdaptiCache decides

Memory is split into a recency side (T1, pages seen once) and a frequency side (T2, pages seen twice or more). The target size of T1 is a single number, p. Two ghost lists, B1 and B2, remember only the numbers of recently evicted pages, so the engine can tell when it evicted the wrong kind of page. Step through 16 references with 3 frames.

0faults, AdaptiCache

0faults, LRU in the same 3 frames

  1. New page: it enters T1. If memory is full, the side that is over its target gives up its oldest page, which becomes a ghost.
  2. Second use: promote to T2. Being used twice is evidence of lasting value, so the page moves to the frequency side.
  3. Further use: stay in T2. It moves to the fresh end of T2, far from eviction.
  4. Ghost in B1: raise p. A once-used page came back soon after eviction. Recency deserved more room.
  5. Ghost in B2: lower p. A frequent page came back soon after eviction. Frequency deserved more room.
